## 2. A-005's path at HEAD: closed and not closed

The path, step by step:
1. The wrapper is killed alone; the engine stays in the wrapper's group.
2. `processAlive(pid)` is false, so the reaper sees the job as `dead` (`reap.ts:34`). After two sightings it
   finalizes `failed` (`:113`).
3. The record, the log line and the wake (`finalize.ts:153`, and the hook's file watch) come first.
4. Only then does the scope get its SIGKILL (`:106`; `linux.ts:313`).

Closed now:
- (a) No process of the job outlives a successful reap. R-026's `endJobScope` SIGKILLs every process of
  `limen-job-<id>.scope`, the engine's group and `setsid` processes included. `test/s9-housekeeping.test.ts:246-247`
  proves this for the orphan, not for the engine.
- (b) The pid is no longer deleted: the record keeps `identity`.
- (c) R-030: prune keeps a finished job's worktree when `git status` lists anything in it or its branch has unlanded
  commits (`pruning.ts:341-358`, `:364-372`). A worker spawn refuses while such a job is unresolved
  (`UNLANDED_WORK`, `spawning.ts:391-407`).

Not closed:
1. The decision reads only the wrapper's identity (`reap.ts:68`, `:93`, `:110`), never the group or the scope.
   `process group gone` does not say what was checked.
2. The order: `failed`, the coordinator's wake and `afterEnd` all come before the kill, so the window is the length of
   `afterEnd`. Inside it, `startGroupAdvance` and the tab closer each await a `startInScope` of up to 10 s
   (`linux.ts:38`). The kill is a SIGKILL only: no TERM, no grace, and no wait (`linux.ts:313`).
3. A failed end of the scope (systemctl unreachable or over its 10 s) leaves only a log line. The reaper passes no
   `onScopeNotEnded`, unlike exhaustion (`wrapper.ts:145-148`), so there is no group KILL fallback. The engine then
   runs under `failed`, and nothing re-checks it: the reaper reads only `running` (`reap.ts:93`).
4. During (2) or after (3), the worktree can be removed or taken:
   - A clean worktree (nothing in `git status`, ignored files included) goes to `limen prune` or another job's setup
     prune. `jobKeeps` is true only for a non-terminal job or an open group (`pruning.ts:109-111`), and the reason
     line is `job failed` (`:457`).
   - `spawn --branch` reuses the worktree, since `liveJobUsesBranch` is false for a terminal record
     (`spawning.ts:798-803`, `:957-965`).
   - `limen abandon` and `limen continue` accept the finished job.
5. A job whose identity is unknown (`born` null) is ended after its confirmation and its scope is killed, even when
   its wrapper runs (`reap.ts:24-26`, `:68`; test `test/u9-process-identity.test.ts:79`).
6. The deploy gate (`seat/deploy.ts:291`) and the migration's refusal (`migration.ts:172`) treat a dead wrapper with a
   live engine as "not running".

## 3. A-056's path at HEAD: a missing or unreadable `born`

- A schema-1 folder has no `born` file. The identity is the record field `{pid, born, bootId}` (`record.ts:82`,
  `:145`), written in the one transition to running with `await processIdentity()` (`wrapper.ts:86-91`;
  `reap.ts:43-47`). It is no longer fire-and-forget.
- A missing `born` or pid (`born` null when `/proc/self/stat` is not `present`) is `unknown`, which is not live, so the
  job is reaped after its confirmation with no grace. Tests: `u9:72` and `u9:79`.
- A null `bootId`, as in migrated records or an unreadable `boot_id`, skips the boot check (`reap.ts:32-33`), so pid
  and `born` alone decide.
- An unreadable stat that is not `ENOENT` is `unavailable` (`linux.ts:145`) and counts as `alive` (`reap.ts:35`).
  While the pid answers `kill(pid, 0)` (EPERM counts as alive, `linux.ts:397`), the job stays `running`
  indefinitely.
- Older folders:
  - `oldJobLive` counts a missing `born` as alive ("a start time it did not record counts as alive",
    `migration.ts:178-179`, `:201-203`). It is used by the deploy gate (`seat/deploy.ts:285-287`) and `folderLive`
    (`:169-175`).
  - The migration nulls a `born` that is not in clock ticks (`:443`), takes the group owner's identity when there is
    no usable pid (`:440-441`), and always writes `bootId: null` (`:441`, `:444`).
- The reaper's own claim has the same shape. A claim entry holds `born` or `""` (`recovery.ts:16`). An entry with an
  empty `born` whose pid is alive counts as a live holder (`:35-40`), so a stale claim under a reused pid blocks every
  reap of that job (`reap.ts:105-108`).

## 8. Migration surface

- On disk: schema-1 folders have no `born` file. Old folders go through `limen migrate`
  (`migration.ts:431-444`, with `bootId` null).
- Record: no new field is needed for "pid stays", since `identity` already stays.
  - A new optional field is additive (D-079).
  - A new state would change `JOB_STATES` (`record.ts:61`), and older readers refuse it (`oneOf`).
  - A new failure detail needs `FAILURE_PATTERNS` (`:608-615`) to keep `process group gone`, since the migration's
    `failureFrom` (`migration.ts:452`) reads old details.
- CLI words: none required. The words that could change are the log line and failure detail
  `failed: process group gone` and the sweep's summary.
- Existing records:
  - Migrated records have no `scope` and a null `bootId`. Finished records from before R-026 have no `scope`.
  - Deploys run with no job running (D-079), but the gate only checks the wrapper (`seat/deploy.ts:291`).
  - A record with no `scope` is ended by `jobScope(id)` (`finalize.ts:119`). For a job that ran unscoped this is
    `is-active` exit 3, so `gone`, and nothing is killed.

## 9. Open design questions (the inventory's list; the ticket settles them or asks)

1. What counts as "gone": the wrapper's pgid (`kill(-pid, 0)`, which misses `setsid` processes, and a pid reused after
   a `born` mismatch may lead a new group), the scope (`is-active`, the cgroup folder, `cgroup.procs` or
   `cgroup.events`), or both.
2. How the reaper ends a live job:
   - TERM then KILL to the group (A-005), with stop's 5 s (`stopping.ts:104`) or A-020's 10 s.
   - A scope TERM, which needs a new platform member and a fake for `--signal=SIGTERM`.
   - `endScope`'s SIGKILL alone.
3. The order: end the scope before `transition` for the reaper only (a new option of `finalizeJob`) or for every
   finisher. The readings must come before the kill (`finalize.ts:69`).
4. An end that fails, or a group that survives KILL: stay `running` with a log line and a retry on the next pass, or
   `failed` with a side failure (T-01). Whether the reaper passes the group KILL as `onScopeNotEnded`.
5. Whether an unknown identity with a live scope still gets reaped. `unavailable` counts as alive forever
   (`reap.ts:35`), and a null `bootId` skips the check (`:32-33`) against A-056's "compares `boot_id`".
6. The log line and failure detail that "state what was checked", and the `reaped` pattern for old records.
7. T-04 and R-032: `status` and `show` already wait 10 s, and a TERM grace adds more. The wake hook's 500 ms tick runs
   a synchronous `systemctl` with up to 10 s per call. Mechanism only, or callers too.
8. Scope of the change: `liveJob` and `ownerState` for every caller (the deploy gate, `folderLive`,
   `BRANCH_LIVE_JOB`, `memberLive`, keeper, pulse), or a reaper-only check.
9. In or out of scope: the recovery claim's empty `born` (`recovery.ts:36-39`), the coordinator registration's
   (`coordinator-registration.ts:133-134`), and `oldJobLive` (`migration.ts:201-203`).
10. Who re-checks a terminal job whose scope end failed (no code does); this ticket or R-032.
11. Tests and seat:
    - Rename `u9` and `s9` (T-29 (6)), and have the fake engine record its pid so that `s9` asserts "the engine is
      dead".
    - Real waits in `s9` versus T-31 (4).
    - A `## Deployment` step for the systemd behaviour (T-28 (5)); the row's Seat column is "—".
12. Whether the unused `query` seam (`reap.ts:22`) and the module cache `gone` (`:17`) stay.
13. Whether prune also refuses a terminal job whose scope is still active (`pruning.ts:109-111`), or relies on the
    reaper's order.
