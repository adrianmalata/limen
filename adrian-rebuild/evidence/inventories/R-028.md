## 1. The job wrapper's signal handlers (A-020's path after R-026)

- `grep -rn 'process\.on("SIG' src hook bin seat` → 1 hit: `src/runtime/wrapper.ts:69` (SIGTERM). There are no
  SIGINT, SIGHUP, SIGQUIT or SIGUSR handlers (0 hits). `bin/limen:15` listens only to `warning`.
- The handler (`wrapper.ts:69-77`) runs `stopRequested = true; stop.abort();` and, unless `exhausted`,
  `shutdownDeadline ??= Date.now() + STOP_GRACE_MS - 500` (the Herdr wake's deadline; `STOP_GRACE_MS = 5_000` at
  `:23`). It signals nothing.
- It is installed after the group check, the scope check (`:52-61`) and the preamble read. A TERM before `:69` gets
  Node's default and ends the wrapper while the job is `starting`; the spawner's `waitForHandshake` then fails it with
  `WRAPPER_NOT_READY` (`src/job/spawning.ts:980-1007`).
- What the flag disarms:
  - `exhaust` returns at once (`:132-135`: `if (exhausted || stopRequested) { return; }`), so the timeout (`:314`), the
    tool cap (`:196-197`) and the stall verdict (`:294`) do nothing.
  - `checkStall` returns before it observes (`:299`), and the 80 % steer returns too (`:318`).
  - The timers are cleared only after the engine's `close` (`:325-328`).
  - `stop.abort()` also ends the start-queue wait (`:95`; `src/runtime/start-queue.ts:213-222`).
- Who receives a TERM:
  - The wrapper is started by `systemd-run` with `detached: true`, so it leads its own session and process group
    (`src/platform/linux.ts:239-253`).
  - The engine is spawned without `detached` (`wrapper.ts:206-210`), so it is in the wrapper's group. The stall
    checks rely on this (`pgid !== process.pid` at `:255` and `:271-277`).
  - A TERM to the wrapper's pid reaches only the wrapper. A TERM to the group reaches the wrapper and the engine; the
    senders are `limen stop` (`src/job/stopping.ts:102`), the exhaustion (`wrapper.ts:140`) and the spawner's
    handshake (`spawning.ts:996`). The exhaustion's own TERM also runs the handler (comment at `:72-73`).
- What is still true after R-026:
  - A TERM to the wrapper alone leaves the engine running with no timeout, cap or stall bound.
  - The reaper sees a live wrapper (`src/runtime/reap.ts:64-69`) and never ends the job.
  - The scope ends only when some finisher runs `finalizeJob` (`src/job/finalize.ts:101-107`).
  - `limen show` prints the wrapper's pid (`src/job/view.ts:94-95`), so a manual `kill <pid>` is still possible. The
    doc line A-020 cites ("Check the recorded PID") is gone since R-021 (`git log -S` → `0e4e588`; 0 hits for
    "recorded PID" in `docs/`).
- The order of the end: `:338` (`stopRequested || result.signal === "SIGTERM" || result.signal === "SIGKILL"`) comes
  before `result.code === 0` (`:342`). An engine that exits 0 by itself after a lone TERM is therefore recorded
  `stopped: process group interrupted`, not `done`.
- A TERM before the engine starts:
  - In the queue, the job ends `stopped` with `record.stopRequested || "stopped while queued"` (`:100-104`).
  - During setup (only for a spawn, `LIMEN_SETUP_KEEP`, `src/config.ts:41-45`), it ends `stopped: stopped during
    setup` (`:110-115`).
  - A TERM after the queue's end and after that setup check, but before the spawn at `:206`, starts the engine with
    every limit already disarmed.
- SIGINT, SIGHUP or SIGKILL to the wrapper alone: there is no handler, so Node's default ends the wrapper. The engine
  runs on in the scope until a reaper run ends the job `failed: process group gone` (kind `reaped`, `reap.ts:113`) and
  the scope's end SIGKILLs it. This is R-027's ground; `test/s9-housekeeping.test.ts:227-236` does it with SIGKILL.
- A stop of the scope from outside (`systemctl --user stop`, or a restart of the user manager, which
  `docs/seat/README.md:80` says "ends every job"): systemd signals every member of the scope (by default SIGTERM, then
  SIGKILL after its timeout; this is systemd's behaviour and is not checked in the tree). The wrapper and the engine
  get TERM together, and today the job is recorded `stopped` with no request.

## 2. How a stop request is recorded and how the end tells it from a signal (A-052's path)

- `limen stop <job> [reason]` (`src/registry.ts:393-412`, refuses `JOB_NO_PID`; `src/commands/stop.ts:9`) calls
  `stopJob` (`src/job/stopping.ts:35-69`).
  - validate refuses a running job with no pid (`:44-54`). The default reason is "stopped by request" (`:53`).
  - apply, under the job's lock, only appends the log line `stop requested: <reason>` (`:56-61`).
- What `endJob` does after apply (`:72-122`):
  - A `starting` job is finalized from `starting` at once (`:74-79`).
  - A running job with no pid yet fails with `JOB_PID_PENDING`, class `transient` (`:84-93`).
  - A queued job gets the record field `stopRequested` through `transition` (`:95-98`). This is the field's only
    writer: 10 hits for `stopRequested` in `src/` outside `record.ts`, all in `wrapper.ts`, `stopping.ts` and
    `migration.ts`.
  - Then: TERM to the wrapper's group (`:102`); a grace task (`waitForProcessGroup(pid, 5_000)`, then KILL and 1 s,
    `:103-109`); a 25 ms turn (2 s when queued, `:111-114`).
  - Last, its own `finalizeJob(jobDir, requestedTerminal(reason), reason, { beforeScopeEnd: () => grace })` (`:115`).
    `requestedTerminal` (`src/job/finalize.ts:28-29`) gives `done` for a reason that starts with `done:`, else
    `stopped`.
- So for a running job that was not queued, the request exists only as a log line, and `record.json` holds no stop
  reason (`failure` is set only for `failed`, `finalize.ts:90`).
  - The field's comment says so: "The reason `limen stop` gave a job that waited in the start queue."
    (`src/job/record.ts:150-151`).
  - The `stop` entry's help text for the reason says "why, kept in the record" (`src/registry.ts:405`), which holds
    only for a queued job.
- Group stop: `stopMemberJob` calls `stopJob(member.id, "group stopped by lead", run.root)`
  (`src/job/group-control.ts:629-640`), the same path. A reserved slot with no process ends `stopped` (`:611-616`).
- Where a signal becomes a requested stop:
  - The wrapper cannot tell `limen stop` from any other TERM, because every TERM sets its flag (§1).
  - Its end (`wrapper.ts:338-339`) runs `finalizeJob(jobDir, "stopped", "process group interrupted", {
    shutdownDeadline })` for the flag or for a SIGTERM or SIGKILL death, an OOM kill included.
  - It reads the record's request only in the queue branch (`:101`) and at the setup check (`:112`).
  - The winner of the terminal transition decides the detail: the stop's reason when `stopJob` wins, "process group
    interrupted" when the wrapper wins. A `done:` stop that the wrapper wins ends `stopped` (A-019).
- Writers of `stopped`: 3 in the wrapper (`:103` through `requestedTerminal`, `:113`, `:339`), 2 in `stopping.ts`
  (`:75`, `:115`) and 1 in `group-control.ts:614`.

## 3. The engine's exit, the terminal state and the failure kinds

- How the end is read:
  - The wrapper takes the engine's end from `child.once("close", (code, signal) => …)` and `child.once("error", …)`
    (`wrapper.ts:218-225`).
  - `startInScope` takes the wrapper's own end from `exit` (`src/platform/linux.ts:254`; type `ProcessEnd`,
    `src/platform/platform.ts:39-40`). `waitForHandshake` uses it ("it ended with <signal | exit code>",
    `spawning.ts:1001`).
- The mapping at `wrapper.ts:336-351`, in order:
  1. `exhausted`: the exhaustion's own `failed: <reason>` (`:145`).
  2. `stopRequested`, or a SIGTERM or SIGKILL death: `stopped: process group interrupted`.
  3. `result.error`: `failed: <message>`.
  4. Code 0: `done: <engine> exited 0`, or `failed` with the engine's stop reason when `isFailedStopReason` holds
     (`error` or `aborted`, `finalize.ts:25-27`).
  5. Anything else: `failed: worker exited with code <code ?? "unknown">`. Any other signal (SIGSEGV, SIGABRT, SIGINT,
     SIGHUP) becomes "code unknown", kind `exit`, and its name is lost.
- `failInternalJob` (`:353-359`, called from `src/main.ts:71-79`) fails the job with the message of an uncaught error.
- How the kind is set:
  - `finalizeJob` writes `failure: state === "failed" ? failureFrom(detail) : null` (`finalize.ts:90`).
  - `failureFrom` (`record.ts:605-607`) matches `FAILURE_PATTERNS` (`:608-615`, 6 patterns: timeout, tool-cap, stall,
    reaped, exit, and `[/^process group interrupted\b/, "signal"]`). Then comes the stop-reason rule (`:632-634`,
    `quota` or `engine-error`); otherwise the kind is null.
  - The migration uses the same function (`src/job/migration.ts:369`, `:447-453`).
- `FAILURE_KINDS` (`record.ts:65-78`) is A-016's list, 12 names: `quota`, `timeout`, `tool-cap`, `stall`, `reaped`,
  `engine-error`, `exit`, `signal`, `oom`, `queue`, `read-only-violation`, `quota-exhausted`.
  - The validator is `nullOr(oneOf(FAILURE_KINDS))` (`:702-709`). `Failure` is `{ kind, detail, resetAt }` (`:84`),
    with no signal field.
  - No path produces `signal` at HEAD: its pattern needs that detail together with `failed`, and the wrapper passes it
    with `stopped`. `oom`, `queue`, `read-only-violation` and `quota-exhausted` are not produced either.
  - Details with a null kind include "not in its scope: …", the launch failures (`spawning.ts:497`, `:722`, `:1005`;
    `src/job/continuation.ts:579`) and spawn errors.
- The real engine: Pi 1.5.1 runs with `--mode json` (`src/runtime/engine.ts:89`), which is Pi's print mode.
  - Print mode installs its own SIGTERM and SIGHUP handlers
    (`adrian-rebuild/input/vendor/pi-coding-agent/package/dist/modes/print-mode.js:34-54`). On a signal it kills its
    tracked detached children, disposes the runtime and runs `process.exit(143)` (129 for SIGHUP). A second signal
    exits at once.
  - Its bash tool starts each command `detached` (`dist/core/tools/bash.js:75-87`), so tool processes lead their own
    groups and a group TERM does not reach them; Pi's handler kills them (`dist/utils/shell.js:170-184`).
  - Print mode has no SIGINT handler (`dist/main.js:111` is the auth login only).
  - So on the seat, a TERM gives the wrapper `code 143, signal null`. With the wrapper's flag set, that is `stopped`.
    A TERM to Pi alone is `failed: worker exited with code 143`, kind `exit`. A SIGKILL (an OOM kill) arrives as
    `signal: "SIGKILL"` and is recorded `stopped`.
  - `## Engine facts` (`plan.md:1154-1180`) has no entry on signals.

## 8. Migration surface and readers of the kind

- No new kind is needed: `signal` and `oom` are in `FAILURE_KINDS` and read today (`record.ts:73-74`, `:705`), so
  records with them need no schema change.
- `stopRequested` already exists (`record.ts:151`, `:208`, `:783`; a nullable text that the migration fills from
  `stop-requested`, `migration.ts:419`). Writing it for every stop changes values, not shape.
- A new field (a signal name in `failure`, a request time) is a key that older readers refuse: `object()` refuses
  unknown keys. D-079 (`plan.md:976-980`): "a change a reader older than the record cannot read raises the schema
  version".
- Old records:
  - Every signal death written by the older Limen, or since R-021, is `stopped` with `failure: null`; its detail
    (`stopped: process group interrupted`) is only in the log.
  - The migration computes a failure only for `failed` (`migration.ts:369`).
  - Records from before R-026 have no `scope`, so no `oomKills`. The row's Mig. column is —.
- Readers of the kind: 2 hits for `failure?.kind` or `failure.kind` in `src/`, `hook/`, `seat/`, `bin/`.
  - `src/job/statistics.ts:216-225` (`limen stats`, "Failed by kind", ordered by `FAILURE_KINDS`, `:20-21`). Its
    `failed` column (`:127`) counts `failed` only, so `stopped` falls in neither column.
  - `seat/alerts.ts:79-84` (`failed:<kind>`, for `failed` only).
- `limen show` and `status` print no kind. Their reason is `stopReason` or the last `failed:`/`stopped:` log line
  (`src/job/listing.ts:246`, `:293-303`, `:346`; `src/job/view.ts:101-107`).
- These readers treat failed and stopped alike:
  - the wake (`src/job/wake-text.ts:82`; `hook/wake.ts:397`, `:522`);
  - `limen status`'s decide list (`src/project/summary.ts:380-382`);
  - group rounds (`src/job/group-rounds.ts:149-153`) and the group's state mirror (`src/job/group-events.ts:112-116`).
- What moves when an unrequested signal goes from `stopped` to `failed`: one alert per such job, the `stats` counts
  and M-11, and the wake's headline (`is failed`). Members of a group stop stay `stopped` (requested).

## 9. Open design questions (the inventory's list; the ticket settles them or asks)

1. What is a stop request: only `limen stop` and group stop (both go through `stopJob`), or also a TERM to the wrapper?
   A-020's Decision has a TERM'd wrapper "record `stopped`", while A-052 and the ticket's title make "a signal without
   a stop request" a failure. Options: a lone TERM to the wrapper counts as a request; it ends `failed`, kind
   `signal`; it is forwarded and the end is decided from the record alone.
2. Where the request lives, so the wrapper can read it at its end: `stopRequested` written for every running job
   before the TERM (in stop's apply, or a transition after it); the `stop requested:` log line; or a new field (a
   schema change). The field's comment and the registry text follow the choice.
3. How the TERM reaches the engine:
   - the wrapper TERMs its own group (it does already at an exhaustion, `wrapper.ts:140`, and its handler runs again);
   - the engine leads its own group (`detached: true`; the stall's pgid checks at `:255` and `:271-277` change);
   - a scope-wide TERM through a new platform member (`systemctl --user kill --signal=SIGTERM`, which the fake
     `systemctl` does not emulate, `test/plant.ts:180-183`).
4. The KILL after the grace: 10 s (A-020) or today's 5 s (`STOP_GRACE_MS`; `stopping.ts:104`); one constant for
   stop, exhaustion and the wrapper, or several. The KILL either comes from the scope's end, with the group KILL as
   fallback (R-026 Deviation 1), or is a group KILL, which ends the wrapper first.
5. Limits armed during a stop's grace: what wins when the timeout or tool cap fires inside a requested stop's grace
   (`stopped` or `failed`), and whether the first-finisher rule settles it.
6. What "killed by a signal" means for the real engine. Pi exits 143 or 129 on TERM or HUP (§3), so `result.signal`
   is null. Options: the wrapper's own record that it received or forwarded a TERM; codes 128+n read as signals; only
   `result.signal`. A related case: an engine that exits 0 after an unrequested TERM ends `done`, or `failed`
   `signal`.
7. Which signals map to `signal`: only TERM and KILL (today's list), or every `result.signal`. Today SIGSEGV, SIGABRT,
   SIGINT and SIGHUP fall to `worker exited with code unknown`, kind `exit`.
8. The detail and the signal name:
   - Option A, a new detail (A-052 Direction: `failed: engine killed by <signal>`) with a new pattern in
     `FAILURE_PATTERNS`.
   - Option B, the kind passed to `finalizeJob` by its detector (R-029's mechanism, A-016 (1)).
   - Either way, decide whether the pattern `process group interrupted` → `signal` stays for history or goes.
9. Who applies `oomKills > 0`, and to which ends:
   - Who: the wrapper reads `platform.scopeReadings` before it picks the detail; `finalizeJob` turns a `signal`
     failure into `oom` from the readings it already reads; or a function of (detail, readings) in `record.ts`.
   - Which ends: `oom` only for a signal death (the plan's note), or also for `exit` and `engine-error` ends with
     `oomKills > 0`, and for the reaper's `process group gone` (R-027's finisher).
10. A requested stop that the wrapper wins: use the recorded reason with `requestedTerminal`, so a `done:` stop ends
    `done` whoever wins (A-019), or leave the race as it is.
11. TERMs before the engine starts:
    - the queue's "stopped while queued" and setup's "stopped during setup" for an unrequested TERM;
    - the window between the setup check (`:112`) and the spawn (`:206`);
    - a TERM that arrives before the handler is installed.
12. Unrequested TERMs from Limen (the handshake, `spawning.ts:996`, while the job is still `starting`) and from
    systemd (a scope stop or a user-manager restart, which would make every running job `failed` `signal` and send an
    alert for each): intended, or named apart.
13. Seat verification: the row has API — and Seat —, while T-28 (5) asks for a `## Deployment` step for systemd
    behaviour. The fakes cannot show a real OOM kill or Pi's exit on TERM. Options: steps such as a trial job TERM'd
    alone and one OOM-killed, or no step.
14. Test shape (T-28 (4), T-31 (3)):
    - a fake-engine word that kills the engine by a signal, or exits 143 as Pi does, against finding the engine's pid;
    - whether the fake's TERM behaviour becomes Pi-like (T-31);
    - writing `oom_kill 1` into the fake cgroup before the kill;
    - a knob for the grace, or a real 10 s wait.
15. Where R-028 ends and R-029 begins: R-028 adds a kind argument to `finalizeJob` (and R-029 builds on it), or adds
    only a detail pattern (and R-029 replaces it). Either way the null-kind details stay with R-029.
