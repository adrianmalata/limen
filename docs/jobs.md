# Jobs

[README](../README.md) · [Command reference](commands.md)

Use this guide to start, inspect, review, and recover coding jobs.

![A coordinator starts workers and reviewers in separate Git worktrees](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/overment/limen/main/docs/limen.gif)

## How work runs

1. Tell the coordinator the outcome that you want.
2. The coordinator writes or moves the ticket. When the work lands, `limen land` moves the ticket to `done/` and regenerates NOW and PROVEN on `spec/build.md`.
3. The coordinator commits the ticket, so that the worker can see it.
4. The coordinator starts a job.
5. Stay in that conversation. A wake comes when a job ends.

Speak only when something looks wrong, or when the coordinator asks you. The coordinator asks about product ambiguity, a real tradeoff, credentials, or a merge.

### Requests

A good request names the outcome and the first artifact. It does not describe the whole repository. The coordinator changes the request into a short spawn with a `Ticket:` pointer. It does not paste the ticket into the prompt.

### Wakes

The type of coordinator controls how the wake comes:

| Coordinator | How the wake comes |
|---|---|
| **Pi** | The coordinator subscribes its session at spawn. The wake hook then puts the completion into that session. |
| **Herdr, without a Pi session** | The job's record holds the coordinator pane in `origin.pane`. When the job ends, Limen runs `herdr agent prompt` on that pane. Then Limen waits until Herdr sees the pane at work. |
| **Group lead** | A team coordinator's finish reaches the lead as a group event through `hook/group-peer.ts`. When that hook is not live for the lead session, Limen prompts the lead pane through Herdr instead, with the group status and the reload hint. Workers inside a team never wake the lead. |

For a Herdr wake, `limen show <id>` shows the result as `herdr-wake`: `turn observed`, `delivered …, turn not seen` (Herdr sent the text; a busy pane may queue it), `may have reached …; not resent` (Herdr timed out after it may have sent the text), or `not sent …` (Herdr refused before it sent input). Limen sends the wake again only after `not sent`, and stops after two attempts; text that may have reached the pane is never sent twice. A wake up to 96 KiB goes as one `herdr agent prompt` argument, below Linux's 128 KiB limit for one argument. A longer wake goes whole as the same `agent.prompt` request on the Herdr socket that `herdr status --json` names. Herdr 0.9.5 reads at most 1 MiB in one request, and only while the request arrives within 5 seconds. No Herdr request can carry a longer wake, so it is `not sent …: prompt_too_large`; the job record keeps the whole final message.

`limen continue` from a shell with no wake route (no Pi session and no Herdr pane), for example a remote executor, keeps the parent's route: the parent's coordinator gets the continuation's wake.

Both routes carry the same completion facts: the task, branch and repository, stop reason, at most ten commits, the whole final message, and undelivered steers when present. The final message is never cut: the coordinator decides the next step from it. The group lead's result carries it whole too. A job with zero recorded tool calls and no commits says that it produced nothing. Missing evidence files remain absent from the wake; the route-specific next-step instruction may differ.

### Job states

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `done` | The run ended cleanly. The selected engine exited 0, and the last stop reason was not `error` or `aborted`. |
| `failed` | The run failed, and Limen records one failure: its kind, set by the code that detected it, never read back from the text, and its detail. The kinds: `timeout`, `tool-cap` and `stall` when a limit runs out (a group's deadline before the engine starts is `timeout` too); `exit` for a nonzero exit; `signal`, with the signal's name (`killed by SIGTERM`, `killed by SIGKILL`), for a signal with no stop request in the record, and `oom` instead when the job's scope counted an OOM kill; `reaped` for a job whose wrapper Limen found gone; `queue` for a wait in the start queue past its limit; `quota` for the provider's subscription limit or rate limit, with its reset time, which Limen resumes itself (see [Subscription limit](#subscription-limit)), and `quota-exhausted` for that end in a job's third resume; `engine-error` for any other error the engine stopped on, and for a run in which a Limen hook refused a Pi event of the wrong shape (`a Limen hook refused Pi event <name>: <field> …`), unless a limit, a signal, a queue end or `quota` ended it; `launch` when the engine did not start (the wrapper outside its scope, a missing worktree, the engine's spawn error, a failed spawn or group launch, a failed wrapper launch or handshake); and `internal` for an error the wrapper itself threw. The reset time of `quota` is the one of the bridge's `kendex:rate-limit` event when the engine received one, which the job's log names (`rate-limit event: <type>, resets <time>`), else the one the provider's text names in its zone (`resets 3pm (UTC)`: the first such hour after the error); a text without a zone leaves it `null`. A failed job keeps its transcript, and its worktree while that holds unlanded work (see [Finished jobs](#finished-jobs)). |
| `stopped` | A stop request ended the run: `limen stop <id> [reason]`, or a group's stop of its members, writes the reason into the record before its TERM, and the reason is the detail. A reason that starts with `done:` records `done` instead. |

A `done` job whose final "Note check" leaves an item open is `done with open checks`. Limen keeps the `done` state and writes one line for each open item to `openChecks` in the job's record: an item marked not proven, changed, dropped or broken, a proven item with no proof that its command ran in the session and no such file, or a check that was sent and not answered. A command counts only when one recorded bash call ended with exit 0 and joined its simple commands with `&&` alone, and no pipe follows the command; any other call may have skipped it or hidden its failure, and the open line names the reason. The wake lists these lines and asks the coordinator to continue the job, start a review, or accept each item with a reason before it lands. `limen show` and `limen status` show the same state.

Failed rows use the record's `stopReason`, not a later tab-close log line. Rate-limit rows show the HTTP status and approximate retry minutes; `limen show <id>` retains the full provider reason and shows the failure's kind as `failure <kind>`, with `· resets <time>` (or `unknown`) for `quota`. Records without a stop reason use the last `failed:` or `stopped:` log entry.

### The job record

Each job folder `.limen/jobs/<id>/` holds one `record.json` (schema version 1): the state and its transitions, the process that runs the job (pid, start time and boot id, each `null` when unknown), the start and finish times, the failure (`failure`: its kind, its detail, and for `quota` the reset time; a record the migration wrote keeps the kind its old text names, `null` when none does), the last rate-limit event the engine received (`rateLimit`: its type, its reset time and when) and the first refusal of a Limen hook (`refusal`: the event, the field, the message and when), each absent when the job had none, the facts its spawn published (label, branch, worktree, base, role, origin, group, route and the like), the start queue's place, and the job's systemd user scope (`scope`: its unit and cgroup, the memory peak, CPU time and OOM kills its end read there, each `null` when it could not, and `cpuPeakPercent`, the largest CPU share of the whole machine its wrapper measured, `null` until the wrapper ends the job and when another process ends it; a record from before the scope has none, and one from before the peak has no `cpuPeakPercent`), and the markers `landed` (`sha`, the commit that holds the job's work on the target, and `at`) and `abandoned` (`branch`, `commit` and `at`), which a record without them reads without. Only Limen writes it, under the job's lock, from the state it expects, and the first finisher wins; after a terminal state the one change is a marker, written once: `limen land` writes `landed` with its merge, `limen ticket done` with its closing commit, and `limen abandon` writes `abandoned` once its branch holds the whole worktree. `progress.json` beside it holds the wrapper's last activity, last tool and tool count. The content stays in plain files: `task.md`, `log`, `result`, the notes, `start-notice`, `session/`, and the receipt folders `notify/`, `steer/` and `recovery/`. Read a job through `limen show <id>`, and the counts over every job of the project through `limen stats`, never by hand; do not edit the record. `limen stats` reads every record, those of the job archive included but a folder there of a job that never ran (no record, or a `starting` one, which the daily prune moved there), and each job's transcripts from the job's own start, and counts them by route and role: the jobs, how many are `done` and `failed`, the run time from their transitions, and the tokens by class (input, cache write, cache read, output) of every transcript entry that carries a usage, compactions included. Then it counts the lands: a land is a job with a `landed` marker, together with every job its `parent` chain reaches (continuations, continued failed rounds) and every reviewer of its branch, so a job belongs to at most one land. `Lands (…), per land: …` gives their number, the days of the first and the last, and the jobs and the tokens by class per land; `Lead time per land (median): …` gives the median time from a land's first queue entry or start to its `landed` time, and of its parts: its jobs' waits in the start queue, the run times of its jobs but the reviewers, and the reviewers' run times, each run time less the job's own queue wait, which a job spends in the state `running`, so the parts do not overlap. A part the records do not give, such as the queue wait of a migrated record and with it that job's run part, leaves the land out of that part's median, which shows as `—` when no land has the part; a land by `limen ticket done`, whose time is a day at midnight UTC, has no lead time, its record migrated or not. With `--sessions <dir>`, the folder of the project's coordinator sessions (Pi's sessions folder for the project's folder, or a copy of it), `limen stats` also reads every session file directly in that folder: `Coordinator (…)` gives the sessions, their turns (assistant messages), the days of their span and their tokens by class, compactions included, and `Coordinator cache read: …` the cache read per turn, the cache read per land whose time lies within the sessions' span (a land by `limen ticket done` by its day, from the local day of the span's first entry to that of its last), and the median and the largest cache write of the first turn after a land, the first assistant message after the next owner or Limen message that follows a `limen land` or `limen ticket done` call. A `--sessions` folder that does not exist exits 2 with `SESSIONS_NOT_FOUND`; without the flag, `limen stats` reads no file outside the project. Then it counts the failed jobs by kind, and names what it did not read: the folders of `.limen/unmigrated/`, a folder with no record, a record or a transcript line that does not parse, a run time the transitions do not give, the lead time of a land by `limen ticket done`, for each part of the lead time the other lands whose part the records do not give, and, with `--sessions`, the lands outside the sessions' span and the session lines that do not parse. A part the record does not hold shows as `unknown`.

**The job's scope.** Every job runs in its own systemd user scope, `limen-job-<id>.scope`. Its wrapper starts there, outside the cgroup of the coordinator's pane, so `--prepare`, the engine, its tools and whatever they start run inside it, a process detached with `setsid` or moved to another parent included, and a restart of Herdr does not end them. Every end of a job, `done`, `failed` or `stopped`, stops the whole scope: each process still in it gets SIGKILL. What the end starts itself, the group's next round and the closer of the job's Herdr tab, runs in a scope of its own, `limen-run-<token>.scope`. While the engine runs, its wrapper reads the scope's CPU time every 3 seconds, also while no tool runs, for the stall rule and for the peak: the largest rate between two readings at least 1.5 seconds apart, as an integer percentage of the whole machine, which the wrapper writes into the record when it ends the job; an engine run shorter than that leaves it `null`. Just before its end, Limen reads the scope's peak memory, CPU time and OOM kills into the record, and `limen show <id>` prints them, the peak CPU share beside the CPU time. A host without the account's systemd user instance runs no job: `limen init` refuses it, and a spawn whose scope does not start exits 4 with `SCOPE_FAILED` and ends its job `failed`.

A project whose job folders an older Limen wrote (one plain file per value, such as `state`) is migrated once: `limen migrate --dry-run` prints what would happen, then `limen migrate` does it, with no job running or starting. Until then every command that reads jobs exits 4 and names `limen migrate --dry-run`, and the coordinator's wake hook stays off and says why. Each folder with a known state becomes a record marked `migrated`, history that `status` and `show` read and that no wake or alert answers; the one-value files it moved, and the files of removed features, go; the content files stay. A folder without a known state moves unchanged to `.limen/unmigrated/<id>/`. The report lists every job, every folder moved with its reason, every file removed, every id of the older 4-hex format and every token two ids share. A token that one job shares only with folders of `.limen/unmigrated/` still names that job, and those folders have no reference; the jobs of every other shared token, and of an older id, are named by their full id. Then the migration marks the finished records that have no marker yet: `landed` where the history of the project's HEAD holds land's closing commit `Close <codes>: landed by <id>`, with that commit and its committer time, and `abandoned` where `limen/abandon/<id>` exists, holds the tip of the job's own branch (as `limen abandon` checks it) and the job's worktree is gone, with the branch's commit and its committer time; it never reads the git graph for a land. The report names each job it marked and each finished job it left without a marker, with why, such as an abandon branch that lacks the tip of the job's branch. A second run has nothing to do. The rollback is the snapshot taken before the migration.

**Neither state is approval.** Neither state means that the ticket is finished or that the branch is safe to merge, because `done` only means that the run exited cleanly. The coordinator reads the record, the diff, and the checks. Then, under the review policy of the project, it merges, or resumes a repair, or asks you.

### Reviews

When a mistake would be expensive, the coordinator starts a new reviewer on the candidate. The reviewer gives a verdict. The reviewer does not change the branch.

## What the coordinator runs

These commands are the harness. The coordinator types them. This list helps you recognize a job ID, a wake, or a recovery step. It is not a daily script.

```bash
# Start a worker
limen spawn --engine <engine> --provider <provider> --model <model> --thinking <level> \
  --label "session handler · F001" \
  'Implement F001: sign-in survives a restart. Start with the failing session test. One commit. Ticket: spec/features/active/F001-auth/ticket.md'

# Ticket pointers accept whitespace after Ticket: and strip closing sentence punctuation.

# Inspect
limen status
limen status --all
limen show <id|token>
git diff HEAD...<branch>

# Review
limen spawn --engine <engine> --provider <provider> --model <model> --thinking <level> \
  --review --detached --branch limen/<job-id> --label "session handler review 1 · F001" \
  'Review the F001 candidate against spec/features/active/F001-auth/ticket.md. Name the commit reviewed.'

# Correct, stop, repair, or continue
limen steer <id> "stay on the session test; do not widen"
limen stop <id> "reason"
limen spawn --engine <engine> --provider <provider> --model <model> --thinking <level> \
  --branch limen/<job-id> --label "session handler repair 1 · F001" 'Focused resume instruction'
limen continue <id> 'Follow-up instruction'

# Follow and land
limen watch <id|token>
# When the work changed a ticket, fix its links on a keeper branch first, then land the keeper job
limen keeper spec/features/active/F001-auth/ticket.md --job <id> \
  --engine <engine> --provider <provider> --model <model> --thinking <level>
limen land <id>
```

### Job IDs and labels

- **Job ID:** The final line of `spawn` carries the durable job ID in `ids.job` ([the final line](commands.md#exit-codes-and-the-final-line)). Spawn prints its text when it ends, the ID after its start line; a spawn killed during setup prints no ID, and `limen status` lists the job its record names.
- **Labels:** A label names the change first and the feature number last. A repair label or a review label names its round.
- **Labels that name a lead:** Ordinary text such as `Fix the lead-in paragraph · F900` is allowed. Lead ownership comes from the coordinator pane, not the label; `--role coordinator` and `--role lead` remain refused.
- **Group labels:** Team jobs read `team-2 coordinator · F773`, so jobs on the same feature remain distinct.

### Steer, stop, and resume

Use these steps in this order:

1. **Steer.** A running job reads a steer between tool calls.
2. **Stop.** Stop a job only after it ignores a steer, or when it is clearly dead. Stop writes its reason into the job's record as the stop request, sends TERM to the job's process group, then KILL when it still runs after a grace of 10 seconds, and then stops the job's scope, which ends every process the job left, a detached one included. Stop a job with `limen stop <id>`, never with a manual `kill` of its pid: the wrapper forwards a TERM to the engine either way, but only `limen stop` records the request, so a TERM without it ends the job `failed`, kind `signal`.
3. **Resume.** A resume uses the same branch and worktree, with the uncommitted files, so the coordinator reads that state first.

### Watch and land

- **Watch:** To take over a job that another coordinator started, watch that job by name. `watch --running` subscribes to every running job, but it is not a takeover.
- **Land:** `limen land` merges a `done` job onto the current branch. Read [Job states](#job-states) first.
- **Ticket check on land:** before it merges, `limen land` checks every ticket the branch adds, moves or changes, at the branch tip, against the plant map's place ids. An error refuses the land and prints the `limen keeper` command that fixes it. A warning prints and does not block. Without a map, land skips the place ids and says so. `limen ticket check <branch>` runs the same check before an ordinary `git merge`.

### Spec keeper

`limen keeper <ticket> (--job <id> [--job <id> …] | --group <id>) [--candidate <branch>]` starts a short detached job (default timeout 20 minutes) that fixes the spec links of finished work: ticket front matter, a planned or dropped board line, and map sources. `--group` adds every member job of that group. The coordinator starts it; no hook does.

- It refuses while a listed job is still running, so it never races a worker. In a group, only the lead starts it.
- It creates `limen/keeper-<fnnn>-<tip>` at the candidate tip: the single job's branch, or `--candidate` for several jobs on one integration branch. The keeper commits only there.
- When the job moved its ticket to another lane, the old path still works: the keeper follows the one folder at the tip with that number and prints `ticket moved: <old> -> <new>`.
- Its task is a packet: the ticket, board and map paths, the candidate, the changed tickets, the current land check, and per job the label, state, branch with base and tip, worktree, session transcript and task. Pi writes the transcript as `.limen/jobs/<id>/session/<time>_<uuid>.jsonl`; the packet names the newest one.
- The keeper edits only `spec/features/**`, `spec/build.md`, and map files that cite a changed ticket. Map edits apply in place and do not land.
- Land the keeper job: it carries the work and the fixes. If the keeper commits nothing, land the original job.

### Finished jobs

Finished jobs keep their records and files in `.limen/jobs/` until the daily retire of `limen seat sweep` moves one marked `landed` or `abandoned` at least 24 hours before to the job archive. The daily prune of the sweep removes the worktrees of finished jobs and prints one line per removal: path, job, reason; a job's start prunes nothing ([Seat sweep](commands.md#seat-sweep)). A resume with `--branch` keeps that worktree.

**Unlanded work stays.** A finished job holds unlanded work when its worktree is there and `git status` lists anything in it (changed, deleted and untracked files; not ignored ones), or when its own branch holds commits that HEAD of the project's checkout does not reach. A review job's branch is its candidate's, so only its worktree counts. The newest job that names a worktree holds it: a continued parent holds nothing, and a member of an open group holds nothing while the group is open. No prune removes a worktree that `git status` shows dirty, whatever job names it, nor the worktree of a job that holds unlanded work, nor a worktree whose HEAD, index or nested checkout records a nested repository at a commit that the project's object store lacks, which lives only in that nested `.git` (whether or not its job is resolved, however clean `git status` shows it), nor a worktree that still holds another registered worktree below it (prune decides the deepest first, so the one below is decided before); each such worktree prints one line, `kept <worktree> (job <id>): unlanded work`, or `kept <worktree> (job <id>): the nested repository <path> holds a commit that the project's repository lacks`, or `kept <worktree> (job <id>): it holds the registered worktree <path>` for one kept only for a registered worktree below it, and so do land and the daily retire for a worktree they keep. A path in such a line has its control characters escaped as JSON writes them, so a name a job chose stays on its line. The work is resolved when it lands, when `limen continue <id>` takes the worktree, or when the record holds the `abandoned` marker, which `limen abandon <id>` writes once `limen/abandon/<id>` holds the whole worktree and no worktree is left; a branch of that name alone resolves nothing. Until each job's work is resolved, a worker spawn in the project exits 1 with `UNLANDED_WORK`, names each such job with its changed files, its unlanded commits and both commands, and names `limen status` in `next`; `limen status` lists those jobs under `Unlanded work`, whatever their age. A worker spawn with `--branch` on the own branch of such a job, the repair spawn of a reviewed candidate, takes over that job's worktree and work, as a continuation does, so only the other jobs make it refuse, when it reuses that job's worktree or the job's worktree is gone; a job whose worktree is there without the branch checked out still makes it refuse. A reviewer, keeper or team coordinator spawn does not wait, since it works on a candidate. A worktree whose `git status` fails is never read as clean: spawn, `limen status`, `limen seat sweep` (its daily prune), `limen abandon` and `limen ticket done` (before its commit) exit 4 with `GIT_FAILED`, and the message names the worktree and its job before Git's own words.

| Command | Effect |
|---|---|
| `limen seat sweep`, its daily prune | Removes the worktrees of finished jobs that hold no unlanded work and prints the path, job and reason of each removal, then one `kept` line for each worktree it keeps; it moves into the archive, never deleting, a record that never started, a folder with no record, a publication's folder left by a spawn, and a folder of the worktree root with no checkout. |
| `limen seat sweep`, its daily retire | Retires each finished job whose record holds the `landed` or `abandoned` marker, at least 24 hours old, and that holds no unlanded work: deletes a landed job's local `limen/*` branch when it holds no commit outside HEAD, then moves the job's whole folder to the job archive. It never decides from the git graph: a job without a marker stays, also one whose branch is at its base, gone, or merged by hand. |
| `limen abandon <id>` | Gives up on a finished job and keeps its work on the branch `limen/abandon/<id>`, then removes its worktree, marks the record `abandoned`, and closes its Herdr tab. |

Only the daily retire of the seat sweep retires records; `limen prune` exits 2 and names `limen seat sweep`. A branch stays while any job that names it is still running or starting, while a checkout other than a finished job's worktree holds it, or while the worktree that holds it is kept: dirty, holding another registered worktree below it, or holding a nested repository whose commit the project's repository lacks. Remote branches stay.

**The job archive.** Retire moves a job's folder, record, `log`, `result`, `session/` and every other file, in one rename to `.limen/archive/<YYYY-MM>/<id>/`, the UTC month of the marker's time, so a retire run again after a stop puts it in the same place and a second run has nothing to do. Before the move, the month's folder is made and its name synced, with the archive's own when it is new, so a crash after the move does not lose the job. It moves under the lock of the token draw, so a new job's token is never one of the archive's, and under the job's own lock, which every write of its record takes; it does not wait for that lock, so a job whose lock another process holds stays for the next daily run. The daily prune moves a record that never started, a folder with no record and a publication's left folder into the archive the same way, to the month of the job's start, else of the move; a folder of the worktree root with no checkout goes to `.limen/archive/unregistered/`, which holds no job. `limen stats` counts the archive's records of jobs that ran, and the GitHub module finds a claim's job there and reads its result and log there for its replies. Every other command reads `.limen/jobs/` only, so `show`, `continue`, `land` and `abandon` of an archived job exit 2 with `JOB_NOT_FOUND`. No Limen command deletes the archive; to free the space, delete a month's folder by hand. A finished job without a marker, such as a review, a continued parent, a group member or a job with no commit, stays in `.limen/jobs/`.

**Abandon a job.** `limen abandon <id>` takes a finished job: it refuses a job that has not finished (`JOB_NOT_FINISHED`, `next` `limen stop <id>`), a member of an open group (`JOB_IN_GROUP`), a job whose worktree a newer job holds (`JOB_CONTINUED`, `next` the abandon of that job), a job with no unlanded work (`NO_UNLANDED_WORK`, `next` `limen seat sweep`, whose daily prune removes what is left), and a job whose worktree is gone while a `limen/abandon/<id>` branch that does not hold the tip of the job's branch exists (`ABANDON_BRANCH_LACKS_WORK`, `next` none: that branch is no save of this job, so a person moves it aside first). It commits the worktree's whole state on top of the worktree's HEAD through a temporary index, so no hook of the project runs and the job's own branch stays where it was; the commit's message is `limen abandon <id>`, with the repository's configured identity. A job whose worktree is gone gets the branch at its own branch's tip. It then removes the worktree as prune does, only while the branch holds all of the worktree and prune itself could remove it. Otherwise the worktree stays, and abandon names it with the cause in a line `kept <worktree> (job <id>): <cause>`: it changed after the commit; a nested repository in it (a submodule, or a folder with its own `.git`) holds commits or changes the commit does not, since the commit records a nested repository only as its checked-out commit, and a nested commit that the project's object store lacks keeps the worktree, also when the worktree's HEAD records it, from abandon and from every prune and land after it (`the nested repository <path> holds a commit that the project's repository lacks`); a branch, a tag, a stash or a reflog entry of a nested repository at a commit the project's object store lacks (`the nested repository <path> holds a branch, tag, stash or reflog entry at a commit that the project's repository lacks`); it holds another registered worktree; Git locks it; or it is the primary checkout, lies outside the worktree root, or is no registered worktree. Once no worktree is left, so that the branch holds the job's whole state, abandon marks the record `abandoned` with the branch, its commit and the time; a worktree it keeps leaves the job unmarked, so its work stays unresolved and listed. Ignored files go with the worktree, which is why abandon cannot be undone. Run again after a stop, it finishes the steps that are left; run on a job whose record holds the `abandoned` marker, it changes nothing, prints `job <id> is abandoned: its work is on <branch> at <commit>` (or says that the branch is gone, or where it points now, when a person deleted or moved it), and exits 0. Limen never deletes a `limen/abandon/<id>` branch, and `limen status` lists each one with its commit under `Abandoned work` until a person deletes it.

### Continue a finished job

To keep the conversation of a finished job, run:

```bash
limen continue <job-id> "Follow-up instruction"
```

Limen copies the saved session into a new linked job. The new job takes the worktree and the work in it, so from then on the parent holds no unlanded work, and the new job keeps the worktree safe from prune. If the old checkout is gone, because it held no unlanded work and prune removed it, or `limen abandon` removed it, Limen restores it at the recorded path from the job's own local branch. A landed job has no local branch left, because `land` and the daily retire delete it; to continue one, first recreate the branch at its landed commit (`git branch <branch> <commit>`). A job that the daily retire moved to the job archive has no reference any more, so it cannot be continued. If Limen cannot create the new job, the parent job and its saved session do not change.

**Only committed branch contents come back.** Prune never removes uncommitted files: their worktree stays until the job is continued or abandoned, and `limen abandon` keeps them on `limen/abandon/<id>`. Recovery is not possible without the branch or the transcript. Limen does not take over a branch that is checked out in a different place.

**One job at a time on a worktree.** `continue` refuses while another job that has not finished runs on the parent's worktree (`LIVE_CONTINUATION`, with `limen show <that job>` in `next`), for every job, a group's or not; once that job has ended, a continuation of the parent starts. Two continuations of one job that check at the same moment never both start: the one that writes second exits 3 with `STATE_CHANGED`.

**Every job can call `finish`.** A continuation keeps its parent's transcript, so the worker may call `finish` again. The handoff becomes the job result, and the job ends `done` when the engine exits. A later reply does not replace that handoff.

### Subscription limit

A job whose engine stops on the provider's subscription limit ends `failed`, kind `quota`, with the reset time ([Job states](#job-states)), and Limen resumes it itself: a step of `limen seat sweep`, which its timer runs every minute, continues the job once the time is 5 minutes past the reset, through the same path as `limen continue`, in the job's session and worktree, on the route its record holds (engine, provider, model and reasoning level), with the default time limit of 90 minutes and the parent's priority. Its task comes from the record: `Limen resumed job <id> after the subscription limit stopped it: resume <n> of 3 (ticket FNNN).`, the reset time, and the line that it goes on from where it stopped; it holds none of the provider's text. The new job's label ends in `· resume <n>`, and its record holds `quotaAttempt`, 1, 2 or 3; a third resume that stops on the limit again ends `failed`, kind `quota-exhausted`, and Limen does not resume it again.

- **Which jobs.** A `quota` job with a known reset time that is the newest record on its worktree, with no `landed` or `abandoned` marker and no `migrated` one, at any age. A member of a group is skipped with the line `not resumed <id>: a member of group <group>, which only its team continues`. A job with no reset time is not resumed yet.
- **Once per reset.** A run prints `resumed <id> after the subscription limit as <new id>, resume <n> of 3`; on the next run the new job holds the worktree, so nothing is due. A resume that another continuation won, such as the coordinator's own `limen continue`, is the line `not resumed <id>: … (LIVE_CONTINUATION)` or `(STATE_CHANGED)` and no failure; a resume that fails to start fails the run with its reason, and the other jobs still go on.
- **The wake.** The completion wake of a `quota` job says, from the record, when Limen resumes it and which resume of 3 that is (`once the reset time is known` without one), or that the 3 resumes were used up, never the provider's text, and it asks the coordinator not to continue the job. `limen continue <id> "<instruction>"` resumes it at once when the owner asks, and the sweep then leaves it to that continuation. `limen show <id>` keeps the provider's text.
- **Owed wakes.** When a coordinator's turn on a wake ends on the limit, read from the provider's error text, the wake hook keeps the wake on the record of its job as `owedWake` (`since`, and `resetAt`, the reset that text named or `null`), and the turn spends neither of the wake's two attempts: a completion wake, an advisory or a start notice alike. The hook delivers no wake of a job whose record owes one; the sweep removes `owedWake` 5 minutes after its reset, or one hour after `since` when no reset was read, and the hook then delivers the job's wakes as any other. A coordinator's own turn that ends on the limit, with no wake in it, wakes nothing.
- **Alerts.** A `quota` failure with no reset time sends `failed:quota` at once. One with a known reset sends nothing while Limen resumes it; it sends `failed:quota` once its resume is overdue by 15 minutes (20 minutes after the reset) while no continuation took over its worktree and it has no `landed` or `abandoned` marker, which covers a member of a group and a resume that fails at every run. `failed:quota-exhausted` comes when the third resume stops on the limit again.
- **Where the tools come from.** The resumed job starts from the sweep's user service, whose PATH is the one of the shell that ran `limen seat sweep --install`; install it from the agents' login shell, so that the job finds `pi`, `node` and `herdr` ([Seat sweep](commands.md#seat-sweep)).

### Start queue

Every plant on the seat shares one machine, so detached jobs wait in one queue for the whole seat, under `~/.limen/start-queue/` of the agents' account. The job's own wrapper waits after its handshake and before prune, `--prepare` and the engine. No daemon runs the queue.

- **Order.** Priority first (`--priority 1|2|3`, 1 the most urgent, 2 the default), then the time the job entered the queue.
- **Priority budget.** A [seat rule](commands.md#seat-wide-rules) with `--start-priority N` holds every detached job less urgent than N until the rule ends: `limen rule set claude-week --until <reset> --start-priority 1 "…"` starts only priority 1 jobs. A held job waits with its own reason, `held by rule <name> until <end> (start priority N)`, one per rule that holds it. Spawn prints it after `queued`, `limen show` shows it after the wait, and `limen seat queue` shows it under the job. A held job never reads the load and never blocks a more urgent job. The hold applies also when the gate is off. When the rule ends, at its end time or by `limen rule end`, the held jobs start in queue order as the gate allows, and each coordinator gets the start notice. Group launches are not held.
- **Budget reading.** Limen does not read the Claude budget; a person reads it and sets the rule. Today the reading comes from the Claude bridge, which receives Claude's rate-limit events: the Pi bridge warns at 80% without a number, and it does not write the reading to a file. Source: `evidence.md` in the F951 feature folder under `spec/features/`, section "The budget reading".
- **Gate.** Only the first job in the queue reads the load: CPU idle across all cores over about two seconds, and free memory: MemAvailable over MemTotal in `/proc/meminfo`. The job starts when CPU is at least 25% idle and memory at least 20% free. A value the machine cannot report does not hold a job back.
- **Settle.** After one start, the next start waits a settle interval of 60 seconds, so the new job's load shows in the next reading.
- **Values.** The seat's file `~/.limen/start-gate.json` of the agents' account changes them for the whole seat: `{ "cpuIdle": 25, "memoryFree": 20, "settle": "60s", "diagnoseAfter": "30m", "failAfter": "2h" }`, and the JSON string `"off"` turns the gate off, which keeps the default `diagnoseAfter` and `failAfter` for a job a rule holds. A `failAfter` that is not longer than `diagnoseAfter` is ignored with it, and both keep their defaults. The file is the only source: `LIMEN_START_GATE` is no longer read, and `limen status` names it when its environment holds it. A waiting job reads the file again on each poll, so an edit reaches jobs already in the queue. The job log names each value that Limen ignores. `LIMEN_START_READING` names a file that holds the reading instead of the machine (`{ "cpuIdle": 10, "memoryFree": 80 }`); tests and manual checks use it, in test mode only ([A job's environment](#a-jobs-environment)).
- **What a coordinator sees.** A job that the gate admits at once and that has nobody ahead starts as before; its log says `start gate: open`. A job that must wait makes spawn print `queued`, shows the pulse `queued` in `limen status` and `limen show`, and logs `queued: …`. When it starts, its log says `start queue: started after …`, and its coordinator gets one start notice on the route its finish uses: the Pi wake hook for a subscribed session, or `herdr agent prompt` to the origin pane in the job's record. The notice says no action is needed. Its receipt is its own (`notify/delivered/_start.<session>`, `notify/herdr-start`) and never counts as the completion wake. The Pi "started" toast waits until the job leaves the queue. A start notice is news only while the job runs: when the job ends before the Pi hook delivers it, no late notice follows, and the session's completion wake carries the notice's line instead. The Herdr route starts its notice as the job leaves the queue, before prune, `--prepare` and the engine; the job does not wait for the pane's answer.
- **Stop and loss.** `limen stop` ends a queued job `stopped` with the stop reason. A queue entry whose wrapper is gone, or whose job ended, does not block the queue: a waiting wrapper and `limen seat sweep` remove it, and `limen seat queue` leaves it out without removing it. A recycled pid can keep a lost entry until the reaper ends its job. A queued job whose folder is removed ends at its next poll: its wrapper leaves the queue and exits, no engine starts, and no terminal record is left, since the folder that would hold it is gone. A queued job whose record the queue cannot check (an error other than a missing file, such as no permission) ends its wait: its wrapper leaves the queue and exits 1, no engine starts, and the job's log holds the cause in a `start queue:` line, when the log can take it.
- **A long wait.** A wait counts from the time the job entered the queue, whatever holds it: the gate, the settle interval, the jobs ahead or a rule. At its first poll after `diagnoseAfter` (30 minutes by default) the job gets one diagnosis: the gate reading, sampled then, the three largest units of the agents' user manager by current memory and the three largest by CPU over the reading's window, each named by its unit, in quotes as data, since a job can name a unit, and its class (`job <id>` for `limen-job-<id>.scope`, `unattributed` for any other unit). The diagnosis goes into the record's `queueWait` and into its advisory, source `queue`, which wakes the job's coordinator once with `queued for <wait> without a start: <diagnosis>`, and into the job's log; the alerts send one line, `<project> queue <job id>`, and the job keeps waiting. At its first poll after `failAfter` (2 hours by default) the wrapper leaves the queue and ends the job `failed`, kind `queue`, with the detail `queued for <wait> without a start: <the last reading>`; no engine starts, and the job is a finished job like any other, with its worktree for `limen continue` or `limen abandon`. The record's `queueWait` (`since`, `diagnosedAt`, `diagnosis`, `leftAt`) keeps the wait after the job leaves the queue, whether it starts, is stopped or fails.
- **Limits.** The job timeout and a group deadline count from engine start; time in the queue never counts against them. Group launches start at once.

### Limits

| Limit | Job |
|---|---|
| Time | 90 minutes (change it with `--timeout 20m`) |
| Tool-start events | 900 (`toolCalls` in the plant limits file) |
| `--prepare` command | 5 minutes (`prepare` in the plant limits file) |
| Stalled tool | 3 minutes of an idle scope and no tool progress (`toolStall` in the plant limits file) |

A prepare command that runs out of time is stopped, and the job log records `prepare failed: timed out`. The job still starts.

A TERM to the job's wrapper disarms none of these limits. The wrapper forwards the TERM to its process group once, and sends KILL to the engine when it still runs after the grace of 10 seconds; a limit that runs out within that grace ends the job with its own failure when it ends the job first.

**Stalled tools.** While a tool runs, the wrapper ends the job `failed`, kind `stall`, with the detail `stalled tool <tool>: scope CPU idle and no tool progress for <n>s`, when for the whole window the CPU time of the job's systemd user scope grew by less than 2 % of the window's wall time and the tool made no progress: no new tool, no output. The window is 3 minutes; `toolStall` in the plant limits file changes it. The scope holds every process of the job, a build detached with `setsid` or left by an earlier tool included, so a tool that waits on a build that works keeps the job, and a lone `sleep` does not. The wrapper, Pi and the bridge's `claude` run in the scope too, so their idle CPU counts: 2 % is the rule's design threshold, above an idle Node process and far below a build, not a measured value. While their idle CPU is above it, the stall rule never ends a job, and the job's time limit does. An engine that waits on the model runs no tool, so the rule never ends it.

**Plant limits.** A plant can set the prepare limit, the stalled-tool time and the tool-start cap in a tracked file, `.agents/limen/limits.json`. Cargo plants use it. Their builds wait for each other: a Mac-wide cargo wrapper that queues builds, or cargo's own lock on a build directory. A prepare command that builds Rust can need more than 5 minutes. A cargo command that waits on a lock whose holder runs outside the job's scope uses no CPU of the scope, so the stalled-tool rule can stop it. A cargo plant commits this file:

```json
{ "prepare": "20m", "toolStall": "15m" }
```

- `prepare` and `toolStall` use the `--timeout` form: `90s`, `20m`, `2h`.
- `toolCalls` is a positive integer: the job ends after that many tool starts.
- The file is the only source of these limits. `LIMEN_PREPARE_MS`, `LIMEN_TOOL_STALL_MS` and `LIMEN_MAX_TOOL_CALLS` are no longer read, and `limen status` names each one its environment holds.
- A plant with no file keeps 5 minutes, 3 minutes and 900 tool starts.
- Limen reads the file in the project root each time it uses a limit, so an edit applies to running jobs too.
- The job log names each entry that Limen ignores, such as an unknown key or a value without a unit.
- The file does not change the job time limit.

**Job notes.** A running job's record holds one note, `advisory`, with its text, its time and its source: `queue`, the start queue's diagnosis of a long wait, or `stall`, which the wrapper sets when it cannot read the scope's CPU time while a tool runs, for example `tool stall observation uncertain: scope CPU unavailable: <folder>/cpu.stat is missing`; such a read never ends the job. A record without a source holds the wrapper's note. A running row in `limen status` and `limen show` names that note `advisory`. A healthy running job shows no note. Each coordinator that follows the job gets one wake about a note of each source, so a stall note still reaches a session that heard the job's queue diagnosis. The wrapper's note clears when it can read the scope's CPU time again or no tool runs; the queue's clears when the job leaves the queue.

### A job's environment

A job's wrapper, its engine, the engine's tools and Claude Code see an environment that Limen builds from an allow-list, never a copy of the spawner's (`JOB_PASSES` and `JOB_SETS` in `src/config.ts`). The Claude bridge forwards Pi's whole environment to Claude Code, so this list is the one filter.

- **From the spawner, unchanged:** `HOME`, `PATH`, `USER`, `LOGNAME`, `LANG`, every `LC_*` name, `TZ`, `TMPDIR`, `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR` and `DBUS_SESSION_BUS_ADDRESS`, the user bus through which a job's own spawns and its end start and end scopes. Every `HERDR_*` name reaches the wrapper alone, for the wake at the job's end; the engine never gets one.
- **Limen's names:** the values the spawn passes (the engine gets `LIMEN_JOB`, `LIMEN_JOB_ID`, `LIMEN_CONTEXT_ROOT`, and a group member's `LIMEN_GROUP_ID` and `LIMEN_TEAM_ID`), and the two settings that a job's own commands still read from the environment, `LIMEN_PACKAGE` and `LIMEN_WORKER_MODEL`. `LIMEN_COORDINATOR` and the `LIMEN_GITHUB_*` names of the GitHub poller never reach a job.
- **Set by Limen in every job:** `DISABLE_UPDATES=1`, so Claude Code does not update itself. `GIT_CONFIG_COUNT=1`, `GIT_CONFIG_KEY_0=credential.helper` and an empty `GIT_CONFIG_VALUE_0`: git reads them after every configuration file, and the empty helper resets every helper that a system, global, repository or URL-scoped entry names. `GIT_TERMINAL_PROMPT=0`, so git fails instead of asking for a name or a password. An empty `GIT_ASKPASS`: git asks an askpass program before it reads `GIT_TERMINAL_PROMPT`, and a set `GIT_ASKPASS`, even an empty one, keeps it from running one that a `core.askPass` of any configuration file or `SSH_ASKPASS` names. `GH_CONFIG_DIR=<job folder>/gh-config`, a folder that Limen does not create: it hides gh's configuration files, so gh finds no login in them. gh would still find a login that it keeps in a system keyring, which it reaches through the user bus. `gh auth status` outside a job names where the account's token is stored; in a job it lists no host either way, since the hidden folder holds gh's list of hosts, so `gh api user --silent` failing in a job is the check that no login reaches it. A job's git keeps the account's identity and settings; it has no credential and needs none, since the coordinator fetches.
- **Dropped:** every other name, such as a token, `SSH_*`, `GIT_*`, `PI_*`, `SHELL`, `TERM`, `NO_COLOR` and `XDG_CONFIG_HOME`. Before the wrapper starts, the job's log gets one line with the names the list dropped, sorted and never with a value, for example `environment: dropped 3 names of the spawner: DEMO_TOKEN, PI_SESSION_ID, SHELL`; when nothing is dropped, there is no line. A project tool that needs a dropped name fails with its cause in that line, and a ticket adds the name to the list.
- **`--prepare`** runs with the engine's environment: it is the project's code, as the engine's tools are.
- **Test mode.** Limen reads its test knobs (`LIMEN_HOME`, `LIMEN_CGROUP`, `LIMEN_END_GATE` and the others of `src/config.ts`) only when `LIMEN_TEST_MODE` is `1` in its own environment, and only then does a job's environment carry them, with the tests' `NODE_OPTIONS`, `NODE_NO_WARNINGS`, `GIT_CONFIG_GLOBAL` and `GIT_CONFIG_NOSYSTEM`. The tests and the fixtures of manual checks set the marker; no Limen command does. `limen status` lists neither the knobs nor the marker.
- **The boundary.** A job runs as the same account, so the account's credential files (gh's, the git helper's, Claude's and Pi's) stay readable by a job's process. The environment hides names, gives git no credential helper and hides gh's configuration files; it does not change which files a process can open. A `~/.netrc` is such a file: git's HTTP transport reads it from `HOME`, which passes, so the agents' account holds none.

### Seat notifications

`limen seat sweep`, which its user timer runs every minute, scans registered projects for terminal jobs that nobody heard and for the advisories of running jobs. A running job's notification title names its note, for example `limen: fix login · advisory`. Each event gets one seat notification, also across restarts and concurrent sweeps. Old timestamp receipts stay valid. A note counts per source: once a coordinator heard a note of the job's queue or its stall, the seat sends none for a note of that source, and a new note of a source no coordinator heard sends one again.

If a notification fails in an unclear way, the sweep logs an error and does not send that event again automatically. Seat notifications do not use up coordinator wakes.

## Recovery

The coordinator does this. You need it only if you look at a stuck tab yourself.

| Symptom | Safe next step |
|---|---|
| The job is quiet or repeats itself | Read `limen show <id>`, the log, and the worktree. Stop only on evidence. Then resume with a narrower task. |
| The worker has a real question | Read its durable note. Answer it. Resume the branch. |
| The wrapper is dead, but the state says `running` | `limen show <id>` and `limen status` say `wrapper dead; limen-sweep will finalize` and change nothing. The seat sweep's timer finalizes the job within a minute; `limen seat sweep` does it at once. Once the recorded process is gone, the sweep ends the job's scope first (TERM, then KILL after a grace), so none of its processes keeps running, and only then ends the job as `failed`, with what it checked in the failure. A scope it cannot end leaves the job `running` with a `reap: … not ended` line in its log, the next run tries again, and from the third failed run in a row `limen status` lists the job under `Jobs the sweep cannot end` and the alerts send `stuck`. Then resume. |
| A completion wake did not come | Run `limen show <id>` and read Git. The job's record stays the source of truth when a notification does not come. |

## How it works

You do not need these mechanics to run jobs. They explain the notes and records above.

- **Publication:** how spawn and continue create a job. Limen writes the complete starting record in a hidden directory, then publishes it with one rename. A publication failure removes the hidden record and leaves the parent record and saved session unchanged.
- **Seat receipt:** the job folder's `notify/seat`. The sweep writes it before it sends a notification, so one event rings once, also across restarts and concurrent sweeps.
